A Lake
That Breathes
樂以忘憂

01Beginning
啟程

It all began with a cup of coffee.
一切，都由一杯咖啡開始。

In 2010, in Saint Petersburg, I had a cup of Danesi Caffe.
2010 年，我喺聖彼得堡飲咗一杯 Danesi Caffe。

The taste of that coffee stayed with me.
嗰杯咖啡嘅味道，一直留喺我心入面。

Later, I went back to Hong Kong and opened my own café, mymy Caffe.
後來，我返到香港，開咗自己嘅咖啡店——mymy Caffe。

I also travelled to Tibet, India, Siberia, and Nepal.
我亦去過西藏、印度、西伯利亞同尼泊爾。

But Russia was different.
但俄羅斯，對我嚟講有啲唔同。

I went there twice.
俄羅斯我去過兩次。

The second time, in 2025, I chose to experience life on the Trans-Siberian Railway.
第二次係2025年，我選擇體驗西伯利亞火車生活。

Along the way, I read a book about Lake Baikal and was drawn to its beauty.
沿途，我讀咗一本關於貝加爾湖嘅書，就迷上咗她嘅壯麗。

I started to collect information about Lake Baikal, prepare my clothes, and plan the trip.
我開始搜集貝加爾湖嘅資料、準備衣物，同埋計劃行程。

Finally, I arrived at Lake Baikal.
終於，我嚟到貝加爾湖。

02Walking in the Snow at -30°C

The road was completely covered in snow.

The wind was strong, and I had to walk slowly.

I could hear the wind, and I could hear my own breathing.

It was freezing, but I felt very awake.

For a moment, it felt like I was the only person in the world.

03Seeing People Playing on the Frozen Lake

From the window, I saw many people playing on the frozen lake.

It looked like a scene from a movie.

I could hear the wind, and I could hear laughter from far away.

I felt surprised.

It felt like the world had suddenly become quiet, but it was still full of life.

04Trying to Exchange Money

In Listvyanka, I needed to exchange some money.

I went to the bank, but it was closed.

It was a small problem, but at that moment, it felt important.

I had to find another way.

I asked around, and finally, I found a place where I could exchange money.

It was a small moment, but it reminded me that travelling is not always easy.

Sometimes, you have to slow down, ask for help, and find another way.

05Hearing the Lake “Breathe”

The lake was very quiet, but the surface was not flat.

There was drifting snow on the ice, and the wind was blowing it away.

I lay down and pressed my ear against the lake.

I could hear the lake breathing.

It was a deep, slow sound, with bubbles underneath.

I felt very calm.

It felt like I was connected to the lake.

06Walking the South and North Lines

The lake and the ice became one.

In the distance, I saw people hiking.

At sunset, I saw dogs walking with their owners.

The frozen lake at sunset was incredibly beautiful.

I could hear the wind blowing through the snow.

I could see my own footprints in the snow.

Besides the supermarket and small shops, I also went to the post office to send a postcard.

It would take five weeks to arrive safely.

There were no overly commercial shops here.

I could feel that people were living freely and comfortably.

07Returning

On the way back, I kept thinking about the lake.

I thought about the snow, the wind, the frozen surface, and the sound of the lake breathing.

I realised that this trip was not just about seeing a place.

It was about feeling life in a different way.

At Lake Baikal, I learned to slow down.

I learned to listen.

I learned to be present.

The lake taught me that life can be quiet, but still full of power.

08Ending

When I returned, I brought this feeling back with me.

It became part of my life map.

It became part of my stories.

And slowly, it became part of my universe.

From a cup of coffee to a universe, this is how my journey continues.
